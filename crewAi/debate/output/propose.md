**Opening Statement**

The motion that *there should be rules for students who miss class due to emergency circumstances and consequently fail to meet the 75 % attendance requirement* is not only reasonable—it is essential for preserving academic integrity, fairness, and student welfare. Clear, well‑designed policies protect both the institution and its learners, turning inevitable disruptions into manageable, transparent events rather than sources of chaos or inequity.

---

**1. Guarantees Fairness and Consistency**

* *Equal Treatment*: Without explicit rules, decisions about excusing absences become arbitrary, leaving some students advantaged by informal leniency while others are penalised. A codified framework ensures every student is judged by the same standards.
* *Predictable Outcomes*: When students know the exact steps—documentation required, timelines for submission, possible make‑up work—they can plan accordingly. Predictability reduces anxiety and prevents accusations of favoritism.

**2. Encourages Responsibility While Recognising Humanity**

* *Balanced Accountability*: Attendance rules exist to motivate engagement and protect learning outcomes. Allowing a blanket exemption for emergencies would undermine that purpose. Rules that permit documented exceptions preserve the incentive to attend while still accommodating genuine crises.
* *Documented Compassion*: A rule‑based system can differentiate between a fleeting illness and a prolonged, verifiable emergency (e.g., hospitalization, family bereavement). This nuanced approach shows empathy without eroding standards.

**3. Protects Academic Standards and Outcomes**

* *Maintains Learning Continuity*: A minimum attendance threshold ensures students receive sufficient exposure to instructional material. When a student’s attendance falls below 75 %, the institution must intervene—through remedial assignments, tutorials, or alternative assessments—to guarantee mastery.
* *Prevents Grade Inflation*: If emergencies were handled ad‑hoc, instructors might feel pressured to inflate grades for absent students, diluting the value of the credential. Formal rules create an objective, auditable process that safeguards grade integrity.

**4. Streamlines Administrative Processes**

* *Clear Workflow*: A rule set (e.g., “Submit a certified medical/official document within five working days; a maximum of two emergency‑related excused absences per semester”) provides a straightforward administrative path. Staff can process requests quickly, freeing time for teaching and advising.
* *Data‑Driven Decision‑Making*: When absences are logged under a common rubric, institutions can analyse trends—identifying systemic issues (e.g., health crises, transport problems) and allocating resources (counselling, transport subsidies) more effectively.

**5. Supports Student Well‑Being and Retention**

* *Reduces Stress*: Uncertainty about academic standing after an emergency can exacerbate a student’s distress. Knowing there is a pre‑established, compassionate protocol mitigates that stress, contributing to better mental health.
* *Improves Retention*: Students who feel the institution is fair and supportive are more likely to persist to graduation. Formalized rules demonstrate that the school values both academic rigor and the personal challenges students face.

**Proposed Core Elements of the Rule Set**

1. **Eligibility** – Valid emergencies (medical, family death, legal obligations) verified by an official document.
2. **Notification Window** – Students must inform the instructor/administration within 5 working days.
3. **Maximum Allowable Excused Absences** – Up to 2 emergency‑related excused absences per semester without penalty; additional cases reviewed by a committee.
4. **Remediation Path** – Mandatory completion of make‑up work or alternative assessments to cover missed content.
5. **Appeals Process** – Transparent appeal mechanism for disputed decisions, overseen by an impartial academic board.

**Conclusion**

Instituting clear, compassionate, and enforceable rules for emergency‑related absences protects the integrity of the 75 % attendance requirement while recognizing that life’s unpredictabilities cannot be ignored. Such a framework upholds fairness, preserves academic standards, eases administrative burdens, and ultimately fosters a healthier, more resilient student body. For these compelling reasons, the motion should be **affirmed**.
